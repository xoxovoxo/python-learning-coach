# Round 1: exploratory needs research

**Status: planned, not conducted. Target: 3–5 interviews of 15–20 minutes.**

## Research objective and limits

Understand how Python beginners respond to difficulty, how they decide an issue is resolved, and whether an important gap remains after existing help. Explore a potential first user segment before deciding whether to prototype Python Learning Coach.

This is a small, purposive exploratory sample. It cannot establish prevalence, market demand, willingness to pay, product effectiveness, or learning gains. No application or model API integration is needed for this round.

## Recruitment

- Recruit 3–5 adults currently learning introductory Python who completed or attempted an exercise within the last two weeks. Do not require dissatisfaction or AI usage.
- Start with classmates, learning groups, or personal contacts who meet those criteria. Seek both course-based and self-directed learners if feasible, and record the limits of a convenience sample.
- The project owner sends invitations personally. Participation is voluntary; avoid recruitment where people may feel obligated because of grading or authority.
- Suggested invitation: “I’m doing a small portfolio research project about how beginners learn Python. I’d like to hear about your recent learning experience in a 15–20 minute conversation. There is no product to test, no coding test, and no preparation required. Participation is optional.”
- Brief screening: Are you 18 or older? How long have you been learning Python? What exercise did you work on within the past two weeks? Is your learning mainly course-based, self-directed, or both?

## Execution

1. Before recruitment, review the hypothesis and interview guide. Practice timing with a mock interview; do not count mock responses as research evidence.
2. Schedule 3–5 sessions over approximately one week. Obtain permission for anonymized notes, use participant codes, and do not record audio or video. Public quotations require separate permission.
3. Follow the guide, focusing on a recent episode before discussing any concept. Record actual help-seeking steps, consequences, successful alternatives, and missing information.
4. Immediately after each session, complete the notes template. Separate quotes, self-reports, observations, and interpretations. Note any changes to questions between sessions.
5. After the round, compare cases in an evidence matrix and write a short decision memo. Keep raw notes outside this repository; remove identifying information from portfolio material.

## Decision criteria set before interviews

These are qualitative decision aids, not statistical thresholds. Multiple independent, detailed episodes are more informative than agreement with the idea. As a working signal, seek similar evidence from at least two participants; that still warrants further research rather than a claim of validation.

| Direction | Evidence that would support it | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continue investigating the problem | At least two participants describe concrete episodes of getting a solution without resolving understanding, meaningful consequences, and gaps after actual use of existing support. Past efforts to understand or practice strengthen the signal. | Refine the problem statement and investigate the pattern with additional learners; consider a separate manual concept test only afterward. |
| Narrow the scope | The strongest evidence clusters around one learning context, task type, or concept, while other participants are adequately supported. | Choose that provisional segment and recruit more people in it. Explain who is outside the scope. |
| Adjust direction | Concrete difficulties mainly involve another problem, existing alternatives work adequately, or extra understanding checks do not address learners' priorities. | Revise the problem hypothesis or stop this concept. Do not add AI simply to preserve the original idea. |
| Gather more evidence | Accounts are vague, conflicting, strongly influenced by leading questions, or too sparse to distinguish explanations. | Improve questions or recruitment and run another exploratory round. Do not force a positive conclusion. |

## Deliverable and later evaluation

Write a short research memo stating who was interviewed in broad terms, what happened, supporting evidence IDs, contradictory evidence, sample limitations, and the chosen next step. Use only real evidence and permitted de-identified quotations. Distinguish “participants reported” from “we observed” and “we infer.”

If later research justifies a concept test, evaluate usability and willingness to engage separately from educational outcomes. Learning effectiveness would require an appropriately designed comparison, independently attempted new problems, and potentially delayed checks. AI accuracy and hint quality would also require their own evaluation. Round 1 establishes none of these outcomes.
