# Exploratory interview guide

**Length: 15–20 minutes · Audience: adult Python beginners · Stage: user needs validation**

Purpose: understand a recent difficult learning experience and what the participant actually did. Do not introduce the coach concept before exploring their experience. Use neutral follow-ups; skip optional probes to stay within time.

## 1. Introduction and permission — 2 minutes

“I’m exploring how people learn Python and deal with difficult moments. This is not a test of your ability. I haven’t established what the main problem is, and I’m interested in what actually happened, including things that worked well. You can skip any question or stop at any time. May I take anonymized notes? Please avoid sharing names, credentials, or private coursework. I won’t record audio or video in this round.”

If they decline notes, ask whether they want to continue without notes; do not create a participant record without permission. Obtain separate permission before including any anonymized quote in a future public portfolio.

## 2. Learning context — 2 minutes

- What are you learning Python for right now?
- How have you been learning it, and what have you worked on recently?

Optional probe: What does a typical learning session look like?

## 3. Reconstruct the most recent difficulty — 6 minutes

- Tell me about the most recent time you got stuck or were unsure while learning Python.
- What were you trying to do? What did you expect, and what happened instead?
- What did you do first? What happened next?
- Which resources or people did you turn to, if any? How did you choose them?
- What did that help you do? What, if anything, remained unclear?

Optional probes: About how long did this take? What happened if you could not resolve it? If comfortable, can you describe or show a non-sensitive example? Showing material is optional; do not copy it by default.

If no recent difficulty comes to mind, ask them to walk through their latest exercise, including what went smoothly. Do not force a problem narrative.

## 4. Outcome and subsequent use — 4 minutes

- How did that session end?
- How did you decide you were done or ready to move on?
- What happened the next time you encountered something similar, if that has happened?
- Did you do anything afterward to check your understanding? It is fine if you did not.

Optional probes: Can you describe that attempt? What made you choose to check or not check? What evidence told you the help had worked?

## 5. Priorities and close — 2 minutes

- Of the difficulties you have experienced recently, which affected your learning most, if any? What happened because of it?
- What have I missed about this experience?
- May I contact you for a later research session? This is optional and separate from participating today.

Thank the participant. Store any contact details separately from notes. If the participant asks about the idea, explain after the interview that it is an unvalidated concept; mark any resulting feedback as a reaction to a pitch, not evidence of a prior need.

## Interviewer reminders

- Ask “Can you give me a specific example?” or “What happened next?” rather than supplying an explanation.
- Avoid “Would an AI coach help?”, “Do you struggle because AI gives you answers?”, and “Would you use our product?”
- Do not teach, correct code, or defend the concept during the interview. If assistance is requested, offer to address it afterward.
- Distinguish remembered events from visible behavior. Ask about resources without assuming that AI was used.
- Do not treat enthusiasm, hypothetical intent, or one correct answer as proof of demand or learning.

The core sections total 16 minutes; use up to 4 additional minutes for relevant probes.
